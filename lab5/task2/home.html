XAU/USD definition

  • US Dollar: Gold prices generally move opposite to the US dollar.
  • Interest Rates: Lower interest rates can make gold more attractive to investors.
  • Inflation: Rising inflation can increase demand for gold as a store of value.
  • Economic Growth: Global economic conditions influence gold prices.
  • Employment Data: Employment reports can affect interest-rate expectations and gold prices.
  • Central Bank Policies: Interest-rate decisions and central-bank gold purchases can influence prices.

What drives the price of gold?

While gold is always seen as a safe-haven currency in times of financial instability, the precious metal’s price is affected by the overall health of the global economy. Gold prices typically move in contrast to the US dollar. So, factors like GDP growth, inflation, employment data, interest rates and the monetary policies of the world’s more influential central banks all affect the price of gold.

Supply and demand factors and market sentiment do not affect gold as much as they do silver, but traders should still keep an eye on them when trading the yellow metal.

  • Supply and Demand: Gold production, jewelry purchases, and investment demand affect prices.
  • Geopolitical Tensions: Wars and political instability can increase demand for gold.
  • Market Sentiment: Investor confidence and fear influence gold buying and selling decisions.
  • Safe-Haven Demand: Investors often buy gold during financial crises and economic uncertainty.
